### 5. Reference demonstrations (#62)

Four demos, `40f8f6c` / `a9c6c3a` / `8bba84c` / `aad4365`. Each is runnable
from one command and emits a C-compatible artifact. Two are worth recording
for what they caught rather than what they built:

**Audio dynamics (#58)** — the issue's own surface list named `rbj_bandpass`
for the mid EQ band. In *series* that strips everything outside the band;
output level collapsed to 0.49 and the limiter never engaged. It is now the
parallel peaking construction `y = x + (g−1)·bandpass(x)`. The limiter then
needed a second correction: an envelope follower alone cannot hold a ceiling,
because a finite attack reaches a transient's peak only after it has passed —
measured, a 0.8 ms follower read 0.835 where the signal was at 1.832, and a
1.83 peak survived a 0.95 ceiling.

**Vibration cancellation (#60)** — the first parameterization used the
conventional RLS forgetting factor 0.999 and produced spectacular-looking
results. Extending the run length showed `reference` (double) *also* amplifies
by 30.9 dB at 40 000 samples: covariance windup, an algorithmic instability at
every precision. Shipping it as a precision finding would have been a lie. At
0.9995 double is stable indefinitely, which leaves precision as the only
variable — and then the real result appears: `posit_full` runs a flawless
29.9 dB for three seconds and amplifies by 34.8 dB once the machine speed
steps.

## Design decisions worth remembering

**Prototypes supply parameters, not objects.** Three separate bindings hit the
same wall: `DDC` cannot adopt a `PyPolyphaseDecimator`, `DecimationChain`
cannot adopt its stages, and neither could a chain adopt a filter's state.
The reason is the same each time — the Py wrappers hold type-erased
`unique_ptr`s, so the concrete `Impl<T>` is unrecoverable, and the inter-stage
sample type must be `T` rather than the `double` the wrappers marshal at their
boundary. Rebuilding from design parameters is the pattern, and it needed new
`.taps` accessors to be possible at all.

**Strict xfails as an upstream notification channel.** When #204 was found,
four tests were written asserting the *correct* behaviour and marked
`xfail(strict=True)`. They flipped to failures the moment the peer was
rebuilt against the fix — no polling, no reminder, and the failure text names
the issue. Worth the small awkwardness of committing tests that assert
something broken.

**Refuse to export a broken design.** All four demo header exporters refuse
rather than emitting from a diverged, amplifying, or unstable configuration.
A header bound for an actuator loop or a motor controller is the last place to
discover NaN weights or a pole on the unit circle.

## Upstream defects found

Three, all reported with measurements and patches:

- **#203** — the Parks-McClellan designers were not equiripple.
  `design_halfband` capped near 21 dB and got *worse* with more taps (127 taps
  measured −24.7 dB, stopband above passband). Root-caused here to the
  inverse-DCT extraction sampling a half-open interval with uniform weights,
  verified against a known polynomial (4.4e-02 error versus 8.9e-16 for the
  DCT-I form). Upstream found three further defects on the same path,
  including a sign error in `compute_delta()` — **the diagnosis here was one
  cause among four, not the cause.** Fixed in v0.9.0.

- **#204** — `lp_to_bp` and `lp_to_bs` produced constellations that were not
  bandpass and bandstop. `lp_to_bs` emitted *no* notch zeros, so its response
  peaked at the band centre. Fixed in v0.9.0.

- **#207** — `NCO`/`DDC` hold frequency and sample rate at `StateScalar` and
  divide afterwards, so absolute RF rates overflow narrow types; `cf24` and
  `half` returned a silent NaN phase increment. Still open; mitigated here by
  a guard that raises.

## One prediction that was wrong

Closing #78 I wrote that once `design_halfband` worked, the SDR cascade would
get *shorter*, since a working half-band designer needs fewer taps than a
windowed FIR. It does not. A half-band's transition band is structurally
centred on 0.25 and several of that cascade's alias bands land inside it, so
the half-band cascade gives 51.6 dB of alias rejection against Kaiser's 89.9
at the same 208 taps — and narrowing the transition makes it *worse*, since a
tighter spec on a fixed tap count buys nothing. The half-band wins on
multiplies (156 non-zero against 208), which would matter under a looser
alias spec. The demo keeps Kaiser, and the claim is corrected in place.

## The recurring theme

Upstream's v0.9.0 changelog names it independently: every one of those defects
shipped because its tests asserted something weaker than the property that
mattered. The same was true here. The #115 tests checked pole/zero *counts*
and metadata, passed happily against transforms whose responses were wrong,
and #204 stayed hidden until the dashboard actually plotted one. Two of my own
test thresholds were wrong before they were right — measuring Butterworth
rolloff from the −3 dB cutoff instead of between asymptotic decades (off by
exactly 3.01 dB), and peak-normalizing a stopband-only sweep for Chebyshev II,
which measures a correct filter against its own ripple and makes it look
broken.

The demos found more defects than any unit test did, because they exercise
combinations unit tests do not.
